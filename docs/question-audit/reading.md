# Reading question audit: house standard for original tests

Measured on the 69 Cambridge Academic Reading tests (books 7-19, 207 passages) and the 4 Cambridge GT tests (C15), against our originals `gen-r-01..04` and `gen-rg-01..02`. Cambridge material is private; this page records patterns and numbers only. Every new original reading test must satisfy the checklist at the end.

Target for originals: P1 700-880 words, P2 780-900, P3 800-950; never above 1,000. Sentences average 22-24 words; about 14% of words have 9+ letters (ours were 19-23%, i.e. too ornate); capitalised proper nouns in mid-sentence 3-5 per 100 words in P1/P2 (ours 0.5-1.2: too few names); digits 0.6-1.2 per 100 words; quotation marks about 0.9 per 100 words (ours: none). First person is rare (0.3-0.6 per 100) and mostly in P3 or in quoted experts.

### Group types (all 69 tests, counted per group; a test has about 8 groups)

TFNG 78, MCQ 77, YES/NO/NG 62, matching information 52, summary (no box) 47, matching features 44, headings 38, summary with word box 37, choose-TWO 36, notes 29, sentence completion 23, table 20, sentence endings 18, short answer 12, diagram label 7, flow-chart 2.

Share of tests containing: TFNG 78%, YES/NO/NG 68%, headings 51%, choose-TWO 36%. Short-answer questions, flow-charts and diagrams are rare; do not use short-answer more than once per four tests.

Where types sit:
- Passage 1: TFNG (45 groups) with a completion task, usually notes or a table. Most common pairs: notes + TFNG (19 tests), table + TFNG (5), TFNG + summary. 13 questions, 2-3 groups. Paragraphs are normally NOT lettered.
- Passage 2: matching information (31), choose-TWO (26), summary (23), MCQ, headings (16), matching features. Typical: matching info + summary + two choose-TWO; matching info + matching features + sentence completion; headings + TFNG.
- Passage 3: MCQ (45), YES/NO/NG (32), summary with word box (20), matching features, sentence endings. Typical: MCQ + sentence endings + YES/NO/NG; MCQ + word box + YES/NO/NG. Passage 3 is argumentative or discursive; the writer's claims are tested.

Group sizes (mean): TFNG 5.6, YES/NO/NG 5.0, notes 6.2, table 5.7, headings 5.6, matching info 4.9, matching features 4.8, summary 4.7, word box 4.9, sentence completion 4.5, sentence endings 4.3, MCQ 3.2 per group (groups of 3-6 single questions), choose-TWO 2 questions per group.

### Answer distribution
- TFNG/YNNG overall: NOT GIVEN 33%, TRUE 26%, FALSE 25%, YES 18%, NO 16% (NOT GIVEN is the most common key; within a group each of the three answers appears at least once and usually 2-3 times).
- MCQ keys are spread A 55, B 62, C 71, D 60. Never more than two consecutive identical keys.

### Passage layout conventions
- Paragraphs are lettered A, B, C... only when a group needs them (matching information, headings). Otherwise paragraphs are unlettered. A lettered paragraph may contain several blocks.
- Subtitle: about half of passages have a one-line standfirst or a short descriptor (a review byline such as "X reviews Y's book about ..." or a question/summary sentence). Passage 1 titles are usually plain topic nouns ("Nutmeg - a valuable spice", "The thylacine", "Stonehenge").
- Title forms seen: topic noun phrase; question ("Should we try to bring extinct species back to life?"); colon title for Part 3; book-review frame.
- Sources and register: popular-science feature (New Scientist, Scientific American), Economist-like analysis, history/archaeology magazine, book review, interview/expert commentary. Expert quotes in single quotation marks with name, institution and role are frequent in P2/P3.
- Content density: named people with affiliation, places, dates, quantities. Specific, checkable facts that questions can paraphrase.

### Exact instruction wording (as in the books, en dash in ranges)
Always begin with `Questions N-M.` (en dash, "Questions 5 and 6." for two) and use these dominant forms:

- TFNG: `Do the following statements agree with the information given in Reading Passage 1? Write TRUE if the statement agrees with the information, FALSE if the statement contradicts the information, or NOT GIVEN if there is no information on this.`
- YES/NO/NG: `Do the following statements agree with the claims of the writer in Reading Passage 3?` (or `views of the writer`) `Write YES if the statement agrees with the claims of the writer, NO if the statement contradicts the claims of the writer, or NOT GIVEN if it is impossible to say what the writer thinks about this.`
- Headings: `Reading Passage 2 has seven paragraphs, A-G. Choose the correct heading for each paragraph from the list of headings below. Write the correct number, i-ix.` Group title `List of Headings`. 2-4 more headings than paragraphs (distribution of extras: 4 x11, 3 x9, 2 x9, 1 x5).
- Matching information: `Reading Passage 2 has seven paragraphs, A-G. Which paragraph contains the following information? Write the correct letter, A-G. NB You may use any letter more than once.` (also with "sections"). Options are the bare letters; statements start lower case with a noun phrase ("a reference to ...", "an explanation of ...", "a comparison between ...") and are NOT in passage order.
- Matching features: `Look at the following statements and the list of researchers below. Match each statement with the correct researcher, A, B, C or D. NB You may use any letter more than once.` Statements are NOT in passage order; person list is 3-5 names.
- Sentence endings: `Complete each sentence with the correct ending, A-F, below.` More endings than sentences; sentences in passage order.
- MCQ: `Choose the correct letter, A, B, C or D.` Stems in passage order, 4 options, each about 8 words, all passage-related.
- Choose TWO: `Choose TWO letters, A-E. Which TWO of the following ... does the writer mention?` One shared list of 5 options, two questions numbered consecutively (`Questions 23 and 24.`).
- Word-box summary: `Complete the summary using the list of words, A-H, below.` More words than gaps (typically 6-9 options for 4-6 gaps). Answers are letters.
- Notes / table / summary / sentences: `Complete the notes below. Choose ONE WORD ONLY from the passage for each answer.` Verbs: always "Choose ... from the passage" (not "Write"). Limits seen: ONE WORD ONLY (most), NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS, rarely THREE WORDS, with `AND/OR A NUMBER` when numbers are keyed. Group title (for example the topic of the notes) is shown above the notes.
- Flow-chart / diagram: `Complete the flow-chart below.` / `Label the diagram below.` plus the same Choose line.

### Ordering and wording rules
- TFNG, YNNG, MCQ, sentence completion, notes, summary gaps, sentence endings follow passage order. Matching information, matching features and headings (by paragraph letter order for headings) do not need to.
- Questions paraphrase; they never reuse a passage sentence. A statement is a single clause of 12-22 words. FALSE statements contradict a specific detail (often a swapped quantity, direction, cause or comparative); NOT GIVEN statements are plausible, on topic and use words from the passage, but the claim itself is absent. YNNG items test opinions and claims, not facts.
- Gap answers are single words or short phrases taken verbatim from the passage; notes and summaries paraphrase the source sentence around the gap, so the answer word is the only identical item.
- MCQ distractors reuse real passage content (a true detail applied to the wrong thing, a partial truth, an over-generalisation).
- Difficulty progression: P1 mainly fact location; P3 inference, writer's attitude, paraphrase.

## 3. Our gaps (generated set before this rebuild)

Systemic:
- Passages 912-969 words in 9 of 12 academic sections (Cambridge median 861-891); GT section 2/3 texts ~970-1,030 words (Cambridge 700-800).
- Prose is uniformly generic: no named experts, no quotations, few places and dates, long Latinate words (19-23% words of 9+ letters vs 14%). Invented studies were flagged "fictional" inside the text. Paragraph topic sentences are formulaic and every paragraph is a similar 100-word block.
- Every passage lettered A-H even where nothing refers to letters; 8 paragraphs always.
- Instructions used hyphens instead of en dashes, "Write ... WORDS from the passage" instead of "Choose ...", omitted `Reading Passage N`, omitted `Questions a-b.` on every headings group, used "In boxes ..." boilerplate in one style only, and matching-information options read "Paragraph A" instead of bare letters.
- Statements copy the passage almost verbatim (TFNG 1-3, notes copying whole sentences); NOT GIVEN statements often ask for an unmentioned count/species ("states how long ...", "identifies the fish species ..."), a visible template.
- Short-answer groups of 2-3 questions in 5 of 6 Academic sections (Cambridge: 12 groups in 207 passages).
- Type mix repeated: every test had HEADINGS + MATCHINFO + SENTENCE; no pairing notes/table + TFNG in Passage 1 as in 19 of 69 Cambridge tests (ours put TFNG + notes in R1 only).
- Several MCQ items offered three options only; answer stems restated passage lists; key positions clustered.
- Headings groups always exactly 3 extras and always in paragraph order; flow-chart question objects carried stray text instead of only an answer.
- GT passages were one paragraph per text (3 and 2 paragraphs) instead of many short paragraphs; no real sub-titles/lists; GT instruction wording used Academic "passage" phrasing and short-answer groups appeared in Section 1.

## 4. House checklist for every new original Reading test (Academic)

1. 3 passages, 40 questions, split 13/13/14 (or 13/14/13). Words: P1 700-880, P2 780-900, P3 800-950.
2. Each passage drawn from a distinct field (science, history/archaeology, technology, social science, environment, arts/language). Include named (invented) researchers with institution and role, quotations in single quotes, dates, figures, places. Hook opening. No "fictional", no self-referential notes. British spelling. Avoid the stock phrasing "On balance", "in conclusion", "It is important to note".
3. Paragraph count 5-10 with uneven lengths (60-170 words). Letter them only if a group needs letters. Give the subtitle to roughly half the passages.
4. Type mix per test follows the Cambridge pairings above: P1 TFNG + (notes | table | summary | sentences | flow-chart); P2 two or three of matching information / choose-TWO / summary / matching features / headings / sentence completion; P3 MCQ + YNNG + (word-box summary | sentence endings | matching features). Over a test: TFNG and YNNG appear once or twice each (not both guaranteed), at most one headings group, short-answer at most once per four tests.
5. Instruction text uses the exact templates in section 1 with en dashes, `Reading Passage N`, `Choose ... from the passage`.
6. TFNG/YNNG: 5-8 items per group, ordered through the passage, NOT GIVEN about a third of items, each answer used at least twice, statements paraphrased (no 5+ word run copied from the passage).
7. Headings: 2-4 extras; each distractor is plausible and tied to a real detail but mismatched to the main idea of any paragraph; every heading is topic-level (not a detail). Letters are the bare keys i, ii...
8. Matching information: options are the bare letters; statements are non-sequential; each letter used once unless the stem says NB.
9. MCQ: 4 options of similar length, key not the longest more than 30% of the time, keys spread over A-D, distractors take real passage details.
10. Gap-fill: answer is a verbatim word/phrase from the passage within the word limit; the surrounding clause is paraphrased; the answer is unique in context. Numbers: use `AND/OR A NUMBER` when keyed.
11. Run the gates: `validateLrTest`, verbatim-answer and word-limit check, independent re-solve (deepseek/qwen or a careful manual re-solve) with 100% agreement, and the Cambridge n-gram check (no shared run of 7+ words, no sentence above 0.5 trigram overlap with any Cambridge passage or question).
12. Not generated through an LLM API: write passages and questions by hand with the same standard.

### General Training (set of 3 sections, 40 questions: 14/13/13)
- Section 1: 2-3 short factual texts (consumer advice, adverts, notices, timetables, product comparison panels) 550-720 words in total, split into many short paragraphs with bold headings; groups: TFNG on one text and matching ("For which ... are the following statements true?") or notes.
- Section 2: two work-related texts (health-and-safety guidance, entitlements, job/training descriptions) 650-800 words in total; groups: notes (`AND/OR A NUMBER`), sentence completion, optionally table/matching.
- Section 3: one general-interest article 750-900 words, lettered paragraphs if needed; MCQ, matching information, summary or headings.
- Instructions say "the text" (`Do the following statements agree with the information given in the text?`, `from the text for each answer`).
